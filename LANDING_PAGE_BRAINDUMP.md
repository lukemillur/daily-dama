## Why I'm building this

Whenever I see kendama players that are more skilled than I am I feel compelled to
pick up my kendama and play more to improve my skills. Sometimes, I'll even say to
myself "Okay, I'm only going to practice whirlwind for the next 20 minutes" but
after a few minutes go by the goal has completely drifted out of my mind and I'm
back to doing the tricks I default to. As a result, even though I'm playing more
I'm not necessarily "leveling up" as I am getting better at what I already know.
That's not a bad thing, in and of itself, but it is the main reason why I've been
playing on and off for six years and only just recently, through the use of my
app, forced myself to learn juggle spike.

Kendama is a flow toy. While playing kendama you ideally will end up in a flow
state. The only problem with that flow state is it isn't really conducive to
growth as it is enjoyment. It's a lot of fun to lace the tricks you know where as
it can be quite frustrating to struggle through sesh after sesh without landing
your goal trick for the first time. I'm not sure if this is simply a product of
older kendamas or a lack of inherent ability, but it took me 6 weeks of on and off
practice before I got my *first* lunar even though I felt like I was dedicating a
lot of my play time towards landing the trick for the first time. I feel confident
in saying that had I been using daily dama I would have landed it sooner.

A flow state sesh is a dopamine factory where a trick unlock grind is more
frustrating than anything else. Have you ever landed a trick that you've been
grinding for ages for the very first time only to go another two or three seshes
without hitting it a second time? I know I have. While focusing on learning a new
trick I keep a mental tally on the amount of time between successful attempts as a
means of gauging my progress. I built daily dama as a means of actually capturing
the data so on off days when it feels like I'm not landing anything (when in
reality it's been five minutes) I can go back and see that once upon a time I had
spent three hours of focused practice before landing my first.

It's not lost on me that kendama serves as a beautiful means of getting people
away from screens and into the present moment. Daily dama has no intention of
taking that away from anyone. There are times to flow without intention and there
are times to practice with intention. Daily dama aims to structure the latter.
